--- layout: post title: "An autoprover as a refrigerator (DRAFT)" date: 2026-06-18 summary: "Work, mathematical progress, and a model with a threshold in prover efficiency." tags: "#lean #math #AI #statistical-mechanics" ---

A refrigerator spends work to produce a specified amount of ice in a warm room. An autoprover spends work to obtain specified mathematical results from a library. Can we distinguish the difficulty of that task from the efficiency of the machine, and predict when more computation will produce useful progress?

We first need an operational definition of work and progress. Then we can ask for statistical laws. Below, a deliberately simple model has a transition: improving guidance changes the work needed for distant targets from exponential to linear in their proof distance. The result is proved for that model; whether mathematical proof search exhibits a corresponding transition is an open research question.

Constructions, work, and progress

Fix a mathematical language and a finite starting library \(K_0\). Let \(V\) be a countable set of theorems, definitions, types, tactics, and intermediate judgments, with their local contexts. A directed hyperedge \(P\to v\) records an elementary construction of \(v\) requiring all premises in \(P\). Alternative edges give alternative constructions. We assume at most two premises per edge and fix a resolution at which proposing one step costs one unit of reasoning; verification costs are neglected.

The current library \(K_t\) retains every verified construction. A valid proposal whose premises are known adds its conclusion; an unsuccessful proposal still costs a unit:

\[ K_{t+1}=K_t\cup\{v\}\quad\text{if }P\to v\text{ is valid and }P\subseteq K_t. \]

Otherwise \(K_{t+1}=K_t\). Every known node therefore has a construction history from the same core. The closure of \(K_0\) is everything obtainable by finite such histories; the run explores this fixed set. We do not sample arbitrary nodes and declare them known.

This includes backward reasoning. A checked construction \(A,B\vdash G\) is a conditional result that can be retained while its premises remain goals. Obtaining \(G\) still requires discharging them. An unresolved hole is not a verified result. We treat an inline construction and a named lemma alike when either can be reused.

Initially, progress can mean proving one externally chosen, interesting theorem \(G\). For a broader research objective, choose a finite set of initially unknown targets \(\mathcal T\) and nonnegative values \(w_G\), and count verified achievements:

\[ U(K)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal T}w_G\,\mathbf 1_{\{G\in K\}}. \]

This is a chosen measure of output, not a claim that interest follows from difficulty. If targets overlap in value, use a nonadditive utility instead. Fixing the output avoids counting arbitrary restatements as mathematical progress.

Distance and efficiency

For a reachable target \(G\), let \(d(K_0,G)\) be the minimum number of additional elementary constructions needed to obtain it, allowing reuse of intermediate results. A prover \(\pi\) takes a random number \(\tau_{\pi,G}\) of proposals to succeed, set to infinity if it never succeeds. Its mean work and its construction efficiency are

\[ W_\pi(G)=\mathbb E[\tau_{\pi,G}],\qquad \eta_\pi(G)=\frac{d(K_0,G)}{W_\pi(G)}. \]

For an initially unknown target and finite mean work, \(0<\eta_\pi(G)\le1\): every successful run contains a construction of \(G\), whose length is at least the minimum. The reciprocal is search overhead. This efficiency credits only the chosen target; other results acquired along the way can be credited through \(U\).

Here is the first refrigerator-like equation:

\[ \boxed{W_\pi(G)=\frac{d(K_0,G)}{\eta_\pi(G)}.} \]

At present it is a definition, not a predictive law. Prediction requires a model for the efficiency. Moreover, \(d\) measures the shortest construction, not the reasoning required to discover it. We will obtain a stronger search lower bound in a specified information model below.

Repeated runs estimate the success curve \(F_{\pi,G}(B)=\Pr(\tau_{\pi,G}\le B)\). Its inverse gives the budget for a chosen reliability. For general output \(U\), the analogous work requirement is

\[ C_\pi(u,\alpha)=\inf\{B:\Pr[U(K_B)\ge u]\ge\alpha\}. \]

This asks for progress \(u\) with probability at least \(\alpha\). It is measurable without knowing the complete hypergraph. Full mean work also depends on the unobserved tail: for integer costs, \(W_\pi(G)=\sum_{t\ge0}[1-F_{\pi,G}(t)]\), since a run costs one unit for every time it has not yet finished.

A homogeneous model of guidance

Consider the simplest dependency structure: a complete tree with \(b\ge2\) children per node, truncated at depth \(L\). The root is the fixed known core. Each edge is a valid, unit-cost construction of its child from its parent. Choose the target uniformly among the depth-\(L\) leaves. Every target has the same construction distance \(L\).

The theorem to be proved is specified, but the sequence of constructions that reaches it is unknown. A paid terminal construction reveals whether it proves the target; verification adds no further cost. There is no separate free operation for testing unexplored leaves. Other node descriptions provide no information about the target's location. This is an explicit restriction on what the prover can learn.

Give the prover a guide that marks promising edges. It always marks the path to the target and independently marks each other edge with probability \(q\). Marks are sampled once and revealed locally when a node is constructed, with the root's marks available initially. Smaller \(q\) means better rejection of unhelpful continuations. The graph and target distribution stay fixed as guidance improves.

The guide's local use is included in the unit cost; its acquisition or training is outside this experiment. Never discarding the target path is a strong simplifying assumption. At \(q=1\) the guide supplies no information; at \(q=0\) it identifies the proof path exactly.

Our prover explores marked children depth first and stops on reaching the target. Child orders are uniform and independent of each other and of the guide. It retains all constructions. Returning attention to a known node incurs no new construction cost. Thus even an unhelpful branch contributes verified nodes connected to the core. This tree is a special case of the hypergraph model, with one prerequisite per construction. Expectations below average over targets, guide marks, and search order.

A work law

A node off the target path has an expected \(\mu=bq\) marked children. Each node on the target path has an expected \(a=(b-1)q\) marked children leading away from it. These two quantities combine geometry and guidance.

An off-path branch grows by a factor \(\mu\) per generation in expectation. At depth \(k\), branches could have left the target path at any earlier depth, so their expected total population is \(a\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\mu^r\). Each such node is explored before the target with probability \(1/2\): at its first divergence from the target path, its branch must come first in the random order. Adding over depths gives the exact mean work

\[ \boxed{ W_L(q)=L+\frac a2\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}(L-r)\mu^r, \qquad \mu=bq,\quad a=(b-1)q. } \]

The first term constructs the proof. The second pays for other constructions explored before it.

Surviving branchingMean work as \(L\to\infty\)Efficiency \(L/W_L\)
\(\mu<1\)\(\displaystyle \left(1+\frac{a}{2(1-\mu)}\right)L+O(1)\)Positive limit
\(\mu=1\)\(\displaystyle L+\frac a4L(L+1)\)Decays as \(1/L\)
\(\mu>1\)\(\Theta(\mu^L)\)Decays exponentially, up to a factor \(L\)

Thus the limiting efficiency is

\[ \eta_\infty(q)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\left(1+\frac{(b-1)q}{2(1-bq)}\right)^{-1},&q<1/b,\\[6pt] 0,&q\ge1/b. \end{cases} \]

Every finite-\(L\) cost curve is smooth in \(q\). The limit develops a threshold at \(q_c=1/b\), with efficiency rising continuously from zero as guidance improves past it. A random prover still eventually succeeds on each finite tree. Its cost, however, grows exponentially with the distance of the target.

Finite-size scaling and typical runs

The finite sum follows by differentiating a geometric series:

\[ \sum_{r=0}^{L-1}(L-r)\mu^r= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{\mu^{L+1}-(L+1)\mu+L}{(\mu-1)^2},&\mu\ne1,\\[6pt] L(L+1)/2,&\mu=1. \end{cases} \]

To examine the transition window, put \(\mu=1+s/L\) for fixed \(s\). Dividing the sum by \(L^2\) gives a Riemann sum converging to

\[ \int_0^1(1-x)e^{sx}\,dx= \frac{e^s-1-s}{s^2}, \]

with value \(1/2\) at \(s=0\). The crossover window therefore has width of order \(1/L\) in \(\mu\).

The efficiency threshold also holds for typical runs of this prover. Couple the trees along one infinite target path. At level \(j\), let \(X_j(h)\) count the off-path nodes visited before the correct child, including immediate children and at most \(h\) generations below them. These variables increase with \(h\); their limits \(X_j\) are independent and identically distributed along the path. Their mean is \(a/[2(1-\mu)]\) below threshold and infinite at or above it.

The finite run satisfies \(\tau_L=L+\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}X_j(L-j-1)\). For any fixed \(h\), bound all but the last \(h\) summands below by \(X_j(h)\) and apply the law of large numbers. Then let \(h\to\infty\). Below threshold, bounding above by \(\sum_{j<L}X_j\) gives the matching limit. Consequently, almost surely,

\[ \frac{\tau_L}{L}\longrightarrow \begin{cases} \displaystyle1+\frac a{2(1-\mu)},&\mu<1,\\[5pt] \infty,&\mu\ge1. \end{cases} \]

For this policy, a budget proportional to proof distance can therefore give high reliability below threshold; every fixed proportional budget fails with probability tending to one at or above it.

Could a better search order remove the exponential regime?

Let \(M_L\) be the number of depth-\(L\) leaves whose entire paths are marked. Even if a prover receives every mark for free, the target is uniform among these leaves. To see this, suppose there are \(N\) edges in the full tree and \(m\) marked edges. Given any compatible target leaf, the likelihood of this marking is

\[ q^{m-L}(1-q)^{N-m}, \]

independent of which compatible leaf was chosen. At \(q=0\) or \(q=1\), the same conclusion follows directly. Unmarked paths cannot lead to the target, and our observation model supplies no further clues.

Until success, terminal proposals can only eliminate candidates. Even granting direct unit-cost terminal proposals without paying to construct prerequisites, their expected number is at least \((M_L+1)/2\). Since \(\mathbb E M_L=1+a\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\mu^r\), every policy that reaches the target in this information model satisfies

\[ \mathbb E\tau_L\ge \max\left\{L,\;1+\frac a2\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\mu^r\right\}. \]

Let \(W_L^*(q)\) be the infimum of mean work over policies chosen before the random target and guide are sampled. Combining this lower bound with our prover's upper bound proves

\[ \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\log W_L^*(q)}{L} =\max\{0,\log(bq)\},\qquad q>0. \]

Optimal mean work is linear below threshold and exponential above it. At criticality these bounds leave a gap between linear and quadratic: we have proved quadratic cost only for the specified depth-first prover. These are search bounds under the stated information restriction, not lower bounds for a prover with additional mathematical insight.

What properties of the closure matter?

In this model, \(b\) describes local growth of the closure, while \(q\) describes the guide. Their product controls whether unhelpful search branches die out quickly or proliferate. A denser supply of alternatives requires stronger discrimination. Global node density alone does not specify this growth.

We can make the result parametric in more structure. Suppose branches have finitely many kinds. Let \(B_{ij}\) be the mean number of children of kind \(j\) produced by a node of kind \(i\). Conditional on these children, the guide independently retains each with probability \(Q_{ij}\). The mean surviving offspring matrix is \(M_{ij}=B_{ij}Q_{ij}\). With independent branching, disjoint descendants, and the mixing condition below, the role of \(bq\) is played by the largest eigenvalue of \(M\).

Conditions and derivation for several kinds of branch

Assume the expected row vector of marked off-path children is the same nonzero vector \(a\) at each target-path node. Descendants reproduce independently according to their kind, with mean matrix \(M\). Random sibling order is independent of descendants. Counting generations as above gives

\[ W_L=L+\frac12\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}(L-r)\,aM^r\mathbf 1. \]

Assume \(M\) is primitive: some power has all entries positive. If \(\gamma\) is its largest eigenvalue, Perron–Frobenius theory gives \(aM^r\mathbf1\sim c\gamma^r\) for a constant \(c>0\). Substitution into the sum proves linear, quadratic, or exponential mean work according as \(\gamma<1\), \(\gamma=1\), or \(\gamma>1\). Below threshold the efficiency denominator tends to \(1+\tfrac12a(I-M)^{-1}\mathbf1\).

The structural assumptions matter. For instance, with \(a=(1,0)\) and \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), we have \(aM^r\mathbf1=r+1\). The same exact formula gives \(W_L=L+L(L+1)(L+2)/12\): cubic cost at eigenvalue one. This matrix is not primitive. Merging branches, shared lemmas, and joint prerequisites require further models.

This is a branching-process connection to percolation: guidance thins possible continuations, and a reproduction threshold governs their growth. Related search transitions under a different noise model are proved by Boczkowski, Feige, Korman, and Rodeh. The recent supercritical sharpness theorem concerns independent bond percolation on transitive graphs; it does not establish a threshold for proof hypergraphs. The work laws above follow from the stated search model.

What could actual prover runs tell us?

Keep the foundation, starting library, target distribution, and accounting fixed, and vary the prover or its guidance. Record total reasoning work, successful proof constructions, and the success curves. If minimum distances can be computed for small instances, measure \(\eta\) directly. Otherwise, a discovered proof only gives an upper bound on \(d\); it does not establish absolute efficiency. Two provers on the same target can still be compared through their work ratio, since the unknown distance cancels.

Magma equations offer a controlled starting point: terms use one binary operation, while proofs still require choices of substitutions, rewrites, and intermediates. The Equational Theories Project classifies 22,028,942 non-reflexive implication questions among 4,694 short laws using Lean-checked generating proofs and refutations, extended by transitivity and duality. Positive implications provide targets, but their published edges summarize whole proofs; experiments must recover elementary construction histories. Every imported lemma must be derived from the chosen assumptions. Generalization beyond equational reasoning would need further evidence.

The model suggests measuring how long rejected approaches keep generating plausible descendants, and how that changes with guidance. Testing a threshold requires growing problem families and finite-size comparisons, not just a sudden improvement on one benchmark. The parameters \(b,q\), or \(M\), have not been estimated for real provers here. Correlated errors, alternative successful routes, and lemma reuse may change or remove the transition.

How cold is a theorem?

There are now three distinct quantities: the chosen value of the result, its minimum construction distance from our library, and the work needed to discover a construction under specified prior information. In the model, every target has distance \(L\), yet improving guidance changes the required work from exponential to linear. Search cost therefore cannot be assigned to the theorem alone without fixing the prover's information and capabilities.

The operational analogue of the refrigerator question is \(C_\pi(u,\alpha)\): how much work buys the desired output, at the desired reliability? The distance gives one lower bound; an information model can supply stronger ones. Calling one of these quantities a temperature would require additional structure. We have not derived an entropy, an absolute efficiency limit for mathematical reasoning, or a conversion from reasoning units to joules.

The concrete research question is whether a few measurable properties of mathematical dependency graphs and prover guidance predict these work curves. The branching model demonstrates one possible mechanism and an exact transition. Extending it to proof hypergraphs, and deciding whether it describes actual autoprovers, remains to be done.
